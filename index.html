Cane & Camera

I carry two things. One holds me up. The other keeps me going. I am Vinay Chittora, a field naturalist in Kota, Rajasthan.

I am disabled. My footprints come in threes: left, right, cane. I try to keep the carbon one small. Tap, tap, tap: every bird hears me first, and I collect empty posts. So I bring a hide. It has four wheels. I park and wait. The sun moves. I do not. Sit still long enough and the birds forget the car. And then, sometimes, a shrike sits on a post in front of the rising sun.

At home, hours of photos to find the one. I edit. I post. I wait again. 3 likes. I know all three people. Phone down. Camera up. I knock on doors. Mostly they stay doors. A few people bought me a coffee, and I remember every cup. Some days I carry more than two things. Jobs. Responsibilities. They made me older. But slowly is still forward. Why? Joy. Nothing beats a bird that stays.

Nobody put milestones on my road, so I made a few: a film, Season: Out of Tune; a citizen science project, Biodiversity of MHTR; open-source software, FALCON; and a website, mhtr.in.

What is the rest of this walk for? Conservation. The forest does not care about my species count or my followers. That is why I like it here. No stress. No pressure. The target is simple: be of real use to the wild. I am slow, I know. Birds taught me patience. And I keep going. Sometimes I just sit under a tree, or lie down and let the birds do the flying.
