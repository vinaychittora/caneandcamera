Cane & Camera

I carry two things. One holds me up. The other keeps me going. I am Vinay Chittora, a field naturalist in Kota, Rajasthan.

I am disabled. My footprints come in threes: left, right, cane. Tap, tap, tap: every bird hears me long before I see it, and I have a fine collection of empty posts. So I bring my own hiding place. It has four wheels. I park, switch off and wait. Sit still long enough and the birds forget the car. And then, sometimes, a shrike sits on a post in front of the rising sun.

At home I go through hours of photos to find the one. I edit it, I post it, I wait again. 3 likes. I know all three people. I am better at waiting for birds than at chasing followers. I knock on doors and ask for a chance. Mostly they stay doors. But a few people bought me a coffee, and I remember every cup. Some days I carry more than two things: the jobs, the responsibilities. They have made me older than I am. But slowly is still forward. Why? For the sheer joy of it. Nothing beats a bird that stays.

Nobody put milestones on my road, so I made a few: a film, Season: Out of Tune; a citizen science project, Biodiversity of MHTR; open-source software, FALCON; and a website, mhtr.in.

I have decided what the rest of this walk is for. Conservation. I may never be famous. Fame was never the bird I was after. The target is simple: to be of real use to the wild. My day has not come yet. I am good at waiting. And I keep going. See my work, or write to me at hello@caneandcamera.com.
